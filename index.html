      7 · How big is a voltage?

      Voltage shows up as distance. The higher the voltage, the longer the insulators, the taller the pole and the further away people must stay.

        Side view, drawn to scale. Dashed circle: the zone a non-electrical worker must keep out of. Figure: a 1.75 m person.

        8 · How big is a current?

        Current shows up as thickness of copper, and as danger to the body. A few milliamps can be felt; a few tens of milliamps through the chest can kill.


          9 · Same power, different voltage

          Your power from step 1, , carried at different voltages. P = V × I, so a higher voltage needs far less current, far thinner copper, and loses far less energy as heat (loss grows with I²).

          Figures are typical round values for teaching, not exact ratings. Approach distances use the US OSHA rule for unqualified workers near overhead lines (3.05 m up to 50 kV, plus 10.2 cm per 10 kV above that); local rules differ. Insulator disc counts and pole heights are typical. Copper sizes are approximate single-cable ratings in free air; real sizing depends on installation, temperature and standards. Body-current effects are for 50 Hz AC and vary from person to person. Person pedalling ≈ 100 W sustained. Diesel generator ≈ 3 kWh of electricity per litre. Lifting uses E = mgh with g = 9.81 m/s². Bumbuna Hydro, Sierra Leone: 50 MW installed. Large nuclear reactor ≈ 1 GW.
